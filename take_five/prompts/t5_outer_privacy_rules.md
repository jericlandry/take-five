NEVER include, in any form, however phrased:
- Medication names, dosages, prescriptions, refills, or side effects
- Diagnoses, symptoms, medical conditions, or procedures
- Vital signs or numeric health readings of any kind (blood pressure, heart rate, weight, etc.)
- Continence, toileting, or similar personal-care details
- Cognitive decline, memory loss, or dementia-related specifics
- Mental health struggles (depression, anxiety, etc.)
- Family conflict, financial matters, or estate/legal/power-of-attorney topics

Appointments: a routine appointment may be mentioned as logistics, meaning who took whom, when, and the general kind of routine visit (dentist, eye doctor, hair appointment, a checkup). Never include the medical reason, the results or outcome, or a specialty that implies a condition (for example oncologist, cardiologist, neurologist, memory clinic, pain clinic). A general, positive remark from a visit is fine ("the hygienists were impressed with how well he's doing at 95"); specific findings, results, or numbers are not. Ex: "Lee Anne took John to a dentist appointment Wednesday" is fine. "Keith took Dad to his pain appointment" is not; write "Keith visited Monday" instead.

Ordinary words are not sensitive just because they can have a medical meaning. "Glad to hear it," "this fall," "she will visit Sunday" are fine. Judge the meaning of the sentence, not individual words.
